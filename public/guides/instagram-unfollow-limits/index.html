Instagram unfollow limits (and how to unfollow without tripping them)

Instagram doesn't publish how many accounts you can unfollow per hour or per day, so any exact number you read online is a guess. What we do know is that unfollowing too fast can get you a temporary action block. The safer approach is small batches, real pauses, and stopping the moment Instagram pushes back.

How to unfollow on Instagram without rushing into a limit, step by step

Audit first, protect the accounts you want to keep, and pick a small batch. Leave FollowAudit's pacing at its defaults or slow it down, review the confirmation, then let the queue work one account at a time. If it pauses because Instagram refused an action, wait a few hours before trying again.

  1. Run an audit firstPaste the FollowAudit script into the browser console on instagram.com and select Start audit. Reading your lists changes nothing on your account.
  2. Protect who you want to keepSelect Protect on friends, family, clients or anyone you never want to lose by accident. Protected accounts can't be selected for any action.
  3. Pick a small batchIn “Don’t follow you back”, select a handful of accounts instead of the whole list. A short queue is easier to watch and easier to check afterwards.
  4. Check the pacingIn Settings, the delay between actions starts at 4 seconds and the break after every 5 actions at 5 minutes. You can raise both, up to 120 seconds and 30 minutes.
  5. Review and confirmSelect Review unfollows. The confirmation lists the accounts and warns that the change may not be reversible. Nothing happens until you confirm.
  6. Let it run, and respect a pauseThe queue unfollows one account at a time and shows the time left. If it pauses to protect your account, leave it and wait a few hours before you try again.

How many people can you unfollow on Instagram per day?

Nobody outside Instagram knows for sure. Instagram has never published an official unfollow limit, the threshold probably isn't the same for every account, and it can change without notice. The figures passed around online come from other people's experience, not from Instagram. Treat the real number as unknown and stay well below anything that feels fast.

Why doesn't Instagram publish the limit?

Limits like these exist to slow down spam and automation. A published number would tell bots exactly how to stay one action under it, so platforms tend to keep the line vague and move it when they need to.

So what pace is safe?

There is no pace anyone can call safe with certainty. What helps is behaving like a person: a few accounts at a time, pauses between them, longer breaks, and no marathon sessions. FollowAudit's own settings screen says it plainly: delays reduce speed, but cannot guarantee protection from platform limits.

Do removing followers and unfollowing count the same?

Instagram doesn't say. FollowAudit treats them the same way: both change your account, both go through the same paced, sequential queue after a confirmation, and neither is ever retried automatically.

Does just loading my lists count?

Reading your followers and following changes nothing, but on a big account it is still a lot of requests. FollowAudit loads the lists page by page. If Instagram answers that it is busy (HTTP 429) or has a server error, it waits and tries that page again, up to three attempts, before giving up.

What is an Instagram action block, and what does “try again later” mean?

An action block is Instagram temporarily refusing an action, such as unfollowing, because the account did too much too quickly or looked automated. You usually see a “Try again later” message, and behind the scenes the response often says feedback_required. Many blocks lift on their own after hours or days, but Instagram doesn't say how long.

What does a block look like?

In the app, a pop-up telling you to try again later and that Instagram restricts certain activity to protect its community. On the web, the request can come back with a normal HTTP 200 status but a body that refuses the action, or Instagram may send you to a page asking you to confirm it's you.

What should I do if I get blocked?

Stop. Don't keep tapping Unfollow, don't switch to another tool, and don't log out and back in to get around it. Use Instagram normally for a while and come back later. If Instagram asks you to confirm your identity, do it in the official app or website. Pushing through repeated blocks may lead to longer restrictions.

Is an action block the same as a ban?

No. A temporary block is a warning, not a ban. But ignoring warnings is the kind of behavior that can escalate, and Instagram doesn't publish where that line is either.

How FollowAudit's unfollow queue paces itself

FollowAudit unfollows one account at a time, never several at once. It waits between actions, takes a longer break after every five, and stops by itself as soon as Instagram rate-limits you, refuses an action or asks for a challenge, or after three failures in a row. It never retries an unfollow automatically.

What are the default delays?

4 seconds between actions and a 5-minute break after every 5 actions. You can change both in Settings, from 2 to 120 seconds and from 1 to 30 minutes. Each pause varies by up to 30% so the rhythm isn't machine-perfect, the pause after a failure is twice as long, and no more than 40 actions run in any rolling hour (adjustable from 5 to 200). Before the first action, a check that changes nothing confirms Instagram still accepts the requests. While the queue runs, the panel shows how many accounts are done and roughly how long is left, and if the queue stops or you close the tab, you can resume it later from where it stopped.

When does the queue stop by itself?

Right away when Instagram answers with a rate limit (HTTP 429) or a refusal (400, 401 or 403), sends back a web page such as a login or checkpoint instead of a confirmation, or returns HTTP 200 with a body that refuses the action, like feedback_required. It also stops after three consecutive failures of any other kind, such as a dropped connection. The panel tells you which of the two happened.

Why doesn't it retry a failed unfollow?

Because repeating a refused action is exactly what makes things worse. When one unfollow fails, FollowAudit marks that account as failed and leaves the decision to you. It only sends each unfollow request once.

Can it unfollow someone I protected?

No. Protected accounts can't be selected, are left out when the queue is built, and are checked again right before each action. You can back up the protected list as a JSON file.

Can I stop it halfway?

Yes. Cancel operation stops the queue, and closing or reloading the Instagram tab ends it too. Accounts already unfollowed stay unfollowed, and each result is shown next to the account.

Is it safe? Risks to know before you unfollow in bulk

No tool can make bulk unfollowing risk-free. Instagram's limits are unpublished and change, FollowAudit uses private web endpoints rather than an official API, and Instagram's terms restrict automated access. Pacing and automatic stops lower the risk of a block or ban, but they don't remove it. Use it at your own risk.

  • No guarantee against bansNobody, FollowAudit included, can promise your account won't be limited, blocked or banned. A tool that promises a “safe” daily number is guessing.
  • Unknown, moving limitsThere is no published safe number. A pace that worked last month, or for someone else's account, may not work for yours today.
  • Unfollows may not be reversibleYou can follow someone again, but if their account is private you have to send a new request and wait for them to accept it.
  • Private endpoints and Instagram's termsFollowAudit relies on private Instagram web endpoints that can change or break without warning, and Instagram's terms of use restrict automated access. Running any script against your account is a risk you take.
  • The code runs in your sessionA console script can do anything your logged-in tab can do. Only paste code you trust, and read it first. FollowAudit is MIT-licensed and open on GitHub. Never paste a script a stranger sends you.
